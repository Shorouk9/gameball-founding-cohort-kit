Launching the founding cohort

Gameball is opening its new agentic loyalty product to a small group of founding brands. This kit is how I would pick those brands, get each one live in six weeks, and prove whether it works.

The six-week pilot

One stamp per week. Select a week to see the plan.

Week 1: Kickoff and baseline

Agree what success means before anything is switched on.

  • 60-minute kickoff with the brand's loyalty or CRM owner and an executive sponsor.
  • Agree the 3 metrics the brand cares about most and how each is measured.
  • Capture the baseline: repeat-purchase rate, members' share of orders, and current traffic from AI assistants.
  • Data handover: platform access, member data fields, consent and privacy sign-off (PDPL or GDPR).

Owners

Gameball onboarding lead, brand loyalty owner

Done when the baseline numbers are signed off by the brand.

Week 2: Connect and check the data

Make sure what an AI assistant learns about each member is correct.

  • Connect the brand's loyalty data (tier, points, purchase history) to the new layer.
  • Run a member match check: what share of members can be recognised reliably.
  • Test with internal accounts: does the assistant see the right tier and balance?
  • Prepare a holdout group so results can be compared fairly later.

Owners

Gameball onboarding lead, Gameball engineering, brand IT contact

Done when at least 90% of members match and test accounts show correct balances.

Week 3: Soft launch

Switch on for a small slice and watch closely.

  • Go live for 10 to 20% of members, or one market only.
  • Daily check of errors and data mismatches for the first five days.
  • A shared channel with the brand for anything that looks wrong.
  • Log every product issue within 24 hours, tagged by severity.

Owners

Gameball onboarding lead, brand loyalty owner

Done when five days pass with no wrong balances shown to customers.

Week 4: Full launch and first readout

Open to all eligible members and share the first numbers.

  • Expand to all eligible members, keeping the holdout group untouched.
  • First weekly readout: recognition rate, assistant-driven orders, early lift against the holdout.
  • Flag any brand whose AI-assistant traffic is too small to read results yet.

Owners

Gameball onboarding lead, brand loyalty owner, brand sponsor

Done when the brand has received and discussed its first readout.

Week 5: Optimise

Improve what members and assistants see, and capture the lessons.

  • Adjust rewards and member offers that assistants can see, one change at a time.
  • 30-minute interview with each brand team: what worked, what was hard, what is missing.
  • Group feedback into themes for the Gameball product team.

Owners

Gameball onboarding lead, Gameball product

Done when each brand has one tested change and a feedback interview logged.

Week 6: Results and decision

Decide per brand: expand, continue or stop.

  • Results review with each brand sponsor against the week 1 baseline.
  • Decision per brand using the go/no-go rules below.
  • Draft a case study with every brand that saw a positive result.
  • One-page summary of product feedback for the Gameball team before public launch.

Owners

Gameball onboarding lead, brand sponsor, Gameball leadership

Done when every brand has a written decision and next step.

Why a launch plan matters now

On 3 October 2026, Gameball's CEO opened a waitlist for founding brands to try a layer that passes each customer's relationship with a brand to the AI assistants shopping on their behalf. AI shopping is growing fast, but it is still early: AI-referred retail traffic grew 393% year on year in Q1 2026, while OpenAI scaled back ChatGPT's in-chat checkout in March 2026.

That makes the first cohort a learning exercise as much as a launch. The brands chosen, and how carefully they are onboarded, will decide what Gameball learns before going public.

A balanced cohort

  • 6 to 8 brands, so each one gets a weekly check-in.
  • Mostly existing Gameball clients, to lower setup risk.
  • At least two markets, for example Saudi Arabia and the UAE.
  • At least three categories, and one brand with physical stores as well as online.

The weekly rhythm

The same routine for every brand keeps a small team on top of 6 to 8 pilots at once.

Monday: shared tracker updated with each brand's status and risks.
Mid-week: 30-minute check-in with each brand owner.
Within 24 hours: every product issue logged and sent to the product team.
Friday: one-page cohort summary for leadership.

Lessons from today's onboarding

Gameball's current self-serve onboarding already uses AI assistants to set up programs. Public reviews and help documents point to three failure points that the founding cohort should be designed around.

  • Repeated answers instead of escalationA merchant whose domain verification kept failing received the same copied steps several times. In the pilot, a second identical answer should trigger a hand-off to an engineer.
  • Hidden prerequisitesOn Shopify, customers must be able to create accounts before they can join a loyalty program. The pilot's week 2 checklist should test every prerequisite before launch, not after.
  • A learning curve at the startNew users found settings hard to navigate at first. Founding brands should get a guided first session rather than a self-serve start.
